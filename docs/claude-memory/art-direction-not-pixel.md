On the match3-rpg-basic project, the name "Pixel Dungeon" was picked casually and is not an art direction. The user explicitly rejected a pixel-art redesign (16x16 pixel tiles, pixel font) after playing it, saying it did not look good, and asked instead for more attractive high-resolution tiles, animations, characters and content.

The user later rejected the chibi character look (big head ~1/2 of height, big eyes, blush) as too childish for the theme and asked for "epic" characters. After an art-direction study (tools/character-study.html, generated by tools/make_characters_study.py) comparing a realistic style (head ~1/6), a stylized one (head ~1/4) and a midpoint, they chose the midpoint "style C": head about 1/5 of body height, medium outline weight, dark-fantasy palette, stern faces, helmets/hoods with glowing eyes, heroic stance. New character, monster and item art should follow style C.

The user also rejected a 3D direction (a three.js study with both toon-shaded low-poly models and the style C drawings as cut-outs in a 3D dungeon): the 3D models looked unappealing and had no personality, and they asked to continue with the existing 2D painted system. Do not propose moving the game to 3D again unless the user brings it up.

When making any visual or art decision for this game, aim for polished, smooth, high-resolution visuals (vector/SVG, gradients, lighting, soft animation) rather than retro pixel art or cute chibi, and do not infer a style from the game's name. Confirm the concrete direction with a side-by-side preview before a large art overhaul, since the user judges the result by eye.
